PART 8: SIH JUDGES DEFENSE MASTER GUIDE (20 Toughest Technical Questions)
====================================================================================================

Q1: Where does your data come from, and is it actually real-time?
A1: "ORBITGUARD connects directly to the official Space-Track.org REST API maintained by the 18th Space Defense Squadron (USSPACEFORCE) and CelesTrak GP feeds. Data is ingested as Two-Line Element (TLE) ephemerides and General Perturbation (GP) JSON records. In orbital mechanics, satellites do not transmit live GPS pings to public servers every millisecond; instead, we ingest authoritative orbital state epochs and propagate their exact instantaneous positions in real time using the standardized AIAA SGP4/SDP4 mathematical model."
Codebase Evidence: backend/app/services/tle_service.py (TLEService.fetch_tle_data), backend/app/services/data_providers/spacetrack.py.

Q2: How do you calculate collision probability (Pc), and why not just use miss distance?
A2: "Miss distance alone is misleading because a 1 km miss distance with high positional uncertainty (large covariance) can have a higher collision probability than a 200 m miss distance with sub-meter uncertainty. ORBITGUARD projects the 3D positional covariance matrices of both objects onto the 2D encounter plane (B-plane) perpendicular to the relative velocity vector. We integrate the 2D Gaussian probability density over the combined hard-body collision cross-section disk using the Foster-2D isotropic model, and benchmark it against Akella-Alfriend curvilinear and 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo perturbation sampling."
Codebase Evidence: backend/app/services/risk_service.py (RiskService.calculate_advanced_benchmarks), backend/app/services/conjunction_service.py.

Q3: How does your Conjunction Screening avoid the O(N^2) computational bottleneck?
A3: "Comparing 35,000 objects pair-by-pair would require over 600 million calculations every timestep. ORBITGUARD implements a 3-tier hierarchical spatial sieve: First, a broad-phase altitude shell interval filter ([perigee - buffer, apogee + buffer]) eliminates 98% of non-intersecting pairs in O(N) time while ranking crossing orbital planes. Second, candidate pairs undergo vectorized SGP4 propagation using NumPy array batches (satrec.sgp4_array). Third, local minima are solved using an orthogonal root solver (r_rel · v_rel = 0) with bracketed Secant and Golden Section Search to find exact sub-second TCA in parallel worker threads."
Codebase Evidence: backend/app/services/conjunction_service.py (ConjunctionService.broad_phase_filter, ConjunctionService.refine_tca_exact).

Q4: How is the Collision Avoidance Maneuver (CAM) calculated?
A4: "Our CAM engine solves Gauss's variational equations for impulsive orbital perturbations executed prior to TCA. For an in-track prograde or retrograde burn, Delta_r_in_track = 3 * a * omega * Delta_t * (Delta_v_t / v_orb). We calculate 4 distinct strategies: in-track prograde boost, in-track retrograde deceleration, out-of-plane cross-track inclination shift, and an optimized multi-axis burn. We then compute exact Monopropellant Hydrazine (N2H4, Isp = 220s) mass consumption using the Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation: Delta_m = m0 * (1 - exp(-Delta_V / (Isp * g0)))."
Codebase Evidence: backend/app/services/cam_service.py (CAMService.plan_avoidance_maneuver).

Q5: How do you verify that an avoidance maneuver doesn't cause a secondary collision?
A5: "Whenever a candidate CAM Delta-V is computed, the engine calculates the perturbed post-maneuver Keplerian elements (new perigee, apogee, semi-major axis, and inclination). It immediately executes an automated broad-phase secondary spatial sieve against the entire active catalog to verify that the displaced orbit does not penetrate any other active satellite or debris buffer zones, outputting a secondary_conjunctions_safe verification flag."
Codebase Evidence: backend/app/services/cam_service.py (CAMService._screen_secondary_conjunctions).

Q6: What is a CCSDS 508.0-B-1 CDM, and why did you implement it?
A6: "CCSDS 508.0-B-1 is the international Blue Book standard for Conjunction Data Messages established by the Consultative Committee for Space Data Systems. It is the universal data format used by NASA CARA, ESA Space Debris Office, and ISRO NETRA to exchange collision risk data between independent operators. ORBITGUARD automatically formats encounter parameters, state vectors in the TEME/WGS84 frame, and covariance matrices into compliant XML and Keyword-Value Notation (KVN) files, complete with webhook dispatching."
Codebase Evidence: backend/app/services/compliance_service.py (ComplianceService.generate_cdm), backend/app/schemas/compliance.py.

Q7: How accurate is SGP4, and what are its physical limitations?
A7: "SGP4 is an analytical perturbation model accounting for Earth oblateness (J2, J3, J4), low-altitude atmospheric drag, and lunar-solar third-body gravitational perturbations. While SGP4 has an intrinsic positional uncertainty of ~1.0 km in LEO over 24 hours, it is the worldwide standard for screening large catalogs.
